SleepSense Library

Clear, practical sleep-health information to help you understand your result.

What is sleep apnea?
Sleep apnea is a sleep-related breathing disorder in which breathing repeatedly becomes interrupted or reduced during sleep. Common warning signs can include loud snoring, witnessed breathing pauses and excessive daytime sleepiness. A screening result cannot diagnose it; persistent symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional.
What is insomnia?
Insomnia involves persistent difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or getting restorative sleep despite having an opportunity to sleep. It can affect daytime energy, concentration and mood. Ongoing or severe sleep difficulties deserve professional evaluation.
Why does stress affect sleep?
Stress can increase mental and physical arousal, making it harder to relax at bedtime and potentially contributing to fragmented sleep. A consistent wind-down routine and stress-management habits can support better sleep.
How much sleep do adults generally need?
Most adults generally benefit from around 7–9 hours of sleep per night. Individual needs vary, so sleep duration should be considered together with sleep quality and daytime functioning.
Why is consistency important?
Keeping a relatively consistent sleep and wake schedule helps reinforce the body's sleep-wake rhythm. Large shifts between weekdays and weekends can make it harder to maintain a stable routine.
What does resting heart rate tell us?
Resting heart rate is one general physiological indicator that can vary with fitness, stress, illness, medications and other factors. It should not be interpreted on its own as a measure of sleep quality or a diagnosis.
